A jar of Vitamin C tablets and a wild amla cost about the same. The factory will tell you they are equivalent. Your grandmother will tell you they are not. Both of them, in their way, are correct.

The tablet contains ascorbic acid. So does the fruit. If "vitamin C" were the whole story, the question would end here. But the amla also contains tannins, polyphenols, pectin, fibre, water held in a particular way, sugars in a particular ratio, a sourness that asks your body to make saliva, a texture that asks your jaw to work. The fruit arrives with an entire small ecosystem; the tablet arrives with one number on a label.

## What we mean by *whole*

A whole food is not the sum of its identified parts. It is the sum of its identified parts, plus the parts we have not yet learned to measure, plus the *order* in which the body receives them, plus the *speed* at which it receives them, plus the *signals* it sends before, during, and after.

When you eat an amla, your saliva starts to flow before the fruit even touches your tongue. By the time the ascorbic acid reaches your stomach, your body has already prepared a different room for it than the one it prepares for a tablet swallowed with water.

## Terroir is not only for wine

A walnut from Shopian is not the same as a walnut from California. The same is true of honey, and ghee, and grapes. The mountain, the season, the bee's mood, the cow's morning — all of it shows up in the food. This is *terroir*, and it is real for every food, not only the ones a sommelier writes about.

If you eat the same brand of biscuit every week, you will taste the same biscuit every week. That is a triumph of engineering and a small loss for your tongue, which was designed to register hundreds of subtle changes a year.

## Why the bee is still here

If sucrose and glucose were the whole story of honey, a factory would have replaced the bee in 1850. It has not. The bee is still here because the bee makes something the factory cannot. The same is true of the cow that makes A2 milk, and the orchard that ripens its fruit in mountain air, and the gwala who churns by hand before sunrise.

This is not a romantic argument. It is a practical one. Your body is nature. It was made for food made by nature. The factory is good at many things; that is not one of them.

— *Ghritam*
